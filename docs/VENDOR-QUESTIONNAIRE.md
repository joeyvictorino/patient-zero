# Questions to ask an AI vendor before they touch patient data (level L1)

Generated by `pz questionnaire --level L1 --overlay nevada`. Do not edit by hand.

35 questions in plain language. For each one: what it means, the question to ask, and what a good answer comes with. A vendor who answers yes but cannot produce the item in the last column has not answered.

How to use it: send the list, ask for written answers with the evidence attached, and ask that the evidence be sealed (`pz seal`) so you can confirm nothing was edited afterward. This list helps you ask better questions. It is not legal advice and it does not replace a Business Associate Agreement or your own counsel.

## Privilege gating

| # | What it means | Ask the vendor | A good answer comes with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (GATE-1) | The product does not touch patient information unless someone deliberately turns that on. | Is patient data off by default in your product, and what exactly turns it on? | A screenshot or setting export showing a new workspace with patient data disabled. |
| 2 (GATE-2) | Patient data is only switched on after a signed Business Associate Agreement, by a named person, with a record kept. | Will you sign a Business Associate Agreement before we send any patient data, and who on your side approves switching it on? | Their BAA template, and a sample of the record made when patient data is enabled. |
| 3 (GATE-3) | Our patients' data is walled off from every other customer's data. | How is our data kept separate from your other customers', and have you tested that one customer cannot read another's? | Results of a cross-customer access test, dated, for the current release. |
| 4 (GATE-4) | If the safety check breaks, the product refuses patient data instead of letting it through. | If your system cannot confirm we have a signed agreement, what happens to a request involving patient data? | A test result showing access is denied when the check fails. |
| 5 (GATE-5) | Parts of the product not approved for patient data warn staff and try to catch patient data entered by mistake. | What stops our staff from pasting patient details into a part of your product that is not covered by our agreement? | The warning staff see, and how flagged entries are handled. |

## Verified chain

| # | What it means | Ask the vendor | A good answer comes with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 (TRUST-1) | The vendor keeps a list of every other company that can receive our patients' data. | Which other companies, including AI model providers, can receive our patient data, and do you have a signed agreement with each? | The current list with agreement status for each company. |
| 7 (TRUST-2) | Patient data can only be sent to approved companies, and the system enforces this, not just a policy. | What technically prevents patient data from being sent to a company that is not on your list? | A comparison of where data actually went during a test against the approved list. |
| 8 (TRUST-3) | AI model companies are barred by contract from training on our patients' data, and we know how long they keep it. | Can your AI provider train on our patient data, and how long do they keep it? | The contract clause, and the retention period in writing. |
| 9 (TRUST-4) | Outside AI assistants cannot connect to patient data unless the vendor has verified which application is connecting. | Can staff connect an outside AI assistant, such as a personal chatbot account, to our patient data? | The list of assistants allowed for patient data, and what happens when an unlisted one tries. |
| 10 (TRUST-5) | Adding a new outside company triggers a risk review before it goes live. | When you add a new AI provider or subcontractor, what review happens first, and will you tell us? | The review record from the last time they added one. |

## Learned context

| # | What it means | Ask the vendor | A good answer comes with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 (MEM-1) | What the AI learns from our patients stays with us and can never surface for another customer. | Does your AI remember things from our data, and could anything it learned appear in another customer's answers? | Results of a test that plants a unique marker in one customer and looks for it from another. |
| 12 (MEM-2) | Our patients' data is not used to train AI models shared with others. | Do you use our patient data to train or improve any model that other customers use? | A written commitment, and the same from their AI provider. |
| 13 (MEM-3) | Anything the AI derives from a patient record can be traced back to that record. | If your AI stores a note or memory derived from a patient record, can you tell which record it came from? | A sample of stored memory showing its source. |
| 14 (MEM-4) | When we delete a record, what the AI learned from it is deleted too. | When we delete a patient record, is everything your AI derived from it deleted as well, and how soon? | The deletion timeline, and a test that searches for a deleted record afterward. |
| 15 (MEM-5) | Saved AI conversations that contain patient information are protected like any other patient record. | Where are AI prompts and replies stored, who can see them, and are they protected as patient records? | Storage location, access list and retention for conversation logs. |

## Audit

| # | What it means | Ask the vendor | A good answer comes with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 (LOG-1) | Every time patient data is viewed, changed, exported or sent, there is a record of who, what and when. | Can you show us every access to a given patient's record, including by AI? | A sample access report for one test record. |
| 17 (LOG-2) | The record of who accessed patient data cannot be quietly altered, even by the vendor's own administrators. | Could one of your administrators edit or delete an entry in the access log without anyone knowing? | How the log is protected, and a verification result. |
| 18 (LOG-3) | Access records are kept long enough to investigate a problem discovered years later. | How long do you keep access logs? | The retention policy. Six years matches HIPAA documentation rules. |
| 19 (LOG-4) | Someone actually reviews the logs, and unusual activity raises an alarm. | Who reviews access logs, how often, and what triggers an alert? | The review schedule and a list of alert rules. |
| 20 (LOG-5) | We can get the access log for our own data whenever we ask. | Can we export the access log for our own data ourselves? | A sample export. |

## Inventory and risk

| # | What it means | Ask the vendor | A good answer comes with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21 (MAP-1) | The vendor knows every place our patients' data is stored, including backups and logs. | Where, exactly, is our patient data stored, including backups, logs and AI memory? | Their inventory of systems holding patient data. |
| 22 (MAP-2) | The vendor has a map of everywhere patient data travels, including to AI models. | Can you show us a diagram of where our patient data goes, including outside your company? | The data flow diagram. |
| 23 (MAP-3) | The vendor reassesses risk every year and before adding any new AI model or partner. | When was your last security risk analysis, and do you redo it before adding a new AI model? | The date and summary of the last analysis. |
| 24 (MAP-4) | Every known risk has an owner, a plan and a deadline. | For the risks you have found, who owns each one and when will it be fixed? | The risk register, with sensitive detail removed. |

## Baseline safeguards

| # | What it means | Ask the vendor | A good answer comes with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 (BASE-1) | Everyone who can reach patient data must use a second login step. | Is multi-factor login required for every user and administrator, with no exceptions? | The setting that enforces it. |
| 26 (BASE-2) | Patient data is encrypted when stored and when moving. | Is our patient data encrypted in storage and in transit, and who holds the keys? | Encryption settings and a transport scan result. |
| 27 (BASE-3) | Every person has their own login. No shared accounts. Idle sessions sign out. | Do you allow shared logins, and how long before an idle session signs out? | Account policy and timeout setting. |
| 28 (BASE-4) | Backups exist, are encrypted, and have actually been restored in a test. | When did you last restore our kind of data from backup as a test, and did it match? | The dated restore test result. |
| 29 (BASE-5) | There is a plan for a breach, and we will be told quickly. | If our patient data is breached, how soon will you tell us? | The notification term in the BAA. The legal limit is 60 days; ask for much less. |
| 30 (BASE-6) | The vendor's staff are trained, have only the access their job needs, and lose it when they leave. | Which of your staff can see our patient data, and how fast is access removed when someone leaves? | Role list and offboarding procedure. |
| 31 (BASE-7) | When the contract ends, our patient data is returned or destroyed, with proof. | What happens to our patient data when we stop using your product? | The return or destruction clause, and a sample destruction certificate. |

## Verification

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 33 (TEST-2) | An independent tester attacks the product at least once a year, including its AI features. | When was your last independent penetration test, and did it cover the AI features? | The summary letter from the testing firm, with the date. |

## Nevada law

| 36 (NV-1) | The AI must never present itself as a therapist or counselor, or as a way to get therapy. Applies to every AI product offered to people in Nevada. | Does your product, its website or the AI itself ever describe the AI as a therapist, counselor or source of mental health treatment? | Marketing copy and in-product text for the AI features. `pz scan --care-claims` checks text for these representations. |
| 37 (NV-2) | The AI must not be built to diagnose, treat or counsel mental or behavioral health conditions. Applies to every AI product offered to people in Nevada. | Is any AI feature designed to assess, diagnose, treat or counsel a mental or behavioral health condition? | A written description of what each AI feature is designed to do. |
| 38 (NV-3) | For therapists and counselors, the AI may help with scheduling, records, billing and notes. It may not take part in care itself. Applies to products sold to mental and behavioral health providers in Nevada. | Which tasks does your AI perform for a behavioral health practice, and does any of them involve the AI interacting with a patient as part of care? | The feature list for behavioral health customers, mapped to the administrative tasks the law names. |
| 39 (NV-4) | A therapist must be able to check AI-written billing and session notes before they are used. Applies to products sold to mental and behavioral health providers in Nevada. | Before an AI-drafted session note or bill is saved or sent, does the clinician see it and approve it? | The review screen, and whether unreviewed AI output can reach the record. |

Applies to health-related data the vendor handles outside HIPAA, such as website visitors, app users who are not yet patients, and wellness features. | Do you collect health-related information about people before they become our patients, and how do you comply with Nevada's consumer health data law for it? | Their consumer health data privacy policy, or their written basis for exemption. |
