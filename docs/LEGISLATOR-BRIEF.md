# AI in health care: ten questions worth asking

Generated by `pz brief legislator --overlay nevada`. Do not edit by hand.

For legislators, agency staff and anyone writing rules or buying for the public. Each question can be put to a vendor, a provider or a witness, and each has an answer that can be checked. They come from an open list of safeguards for AI products that handle patient information. This page takes no position on any bill.

## 1. When a clinic uses this AI product, which other companies receive the patient's information?

**Why it matters.** The product is rarely one company. The AI model, the hosting and the analytics are often three more, and each one holds patient data.

**A good answer comes with.** A written list of every company, with a signed agreement for each, and something technical that stops data going anywhere else.

**Nevada today (Senate Bill 370 (2023), NRS 603A.500 to 603A.590).** Where the vendor handles consumer health data that HIPAA does not cover, it shall state whether it is exempt as an entity subject to HIPAA, and if not, shall publish a consumer health data privacy policy, obtain consent before collecting or sharing, honor requests to stop and to delete, and keep such data out of geofencing around health care facilities.

Safeguards behind this question: TRUST-1, TRUST-2.

## 2. Can the company that makes the AI model keep patient information or train on it?

**Why it matters.** Information used to train a model cannot be taken back out.

**A good answer comes with.** A contract clause that bars training on patient data and states how long anything is kept, and proof that data is de-identified before any reuse.

**Nevada today.** This project's overlay lists no state rule on this question. That is a statement about the overlay, not a finding that no rule exists.

Safeguards behind this question: TRUST-3, MEM-2, CONSENT-8.

## 3. Is the patient told when AI is involved in their care or their messages, and can they say no?

**Why it matters.** People cannot object to what they do not know about.

**A good answer comes with.** The exact notice a patient sees, and a setting that turns AI off for one patient and is honored everywhere.

**Nevada today.** This project's overlay lists no state rule on this question. That is a statement about the overlay, not a finding that no rule exists.

Safeguards behind this question: CONSENT-1, CONSENT-3, CONSENT-4.

## 4. Is anyone recorded or transcribed by an AI tool without having agreed?

**Why it matters.** AI note-takers record visits and calls. Consent rules for recording differ by state and by whether it is a call or in person.

**A good answer comes with.** Everyone is asked before recording starts, the answer is kept, and the visit goes ahead without recording when someone declines.

**Nevada today (NRS 200.620 and 200.650, NRS 200.620, 200.650).** A feature that records or transcribes a phone or video call shall obtain the consent of every party before recording. NRS 200.620 bars intercepting a wire communication without one party's consent plus a court order or emergency, and the Nevada Supreme Court has read it to bar taping a call with only one party's consent (Lane v. Allstate, 1998; extended to cell phones in Sharpe v. State, 2015). It does not reach a recording made entirely outside Nevada (Ditech v. Buckles, 2017). In-person conversations under NRS 200.650 need one party's authorization; CONSENT-2 in the profile asks for every party's agreement in all cases.

Safeguards behind this question: CONSENT-2.

## 5. Does the AI ever act on its own, such as sending a message or changing a record, or does a person approve first?

**Why it matters.** A wrong answer on a screen is a mistake. A wrong message sent to the wrong person is a breach.

**A good answer comes with.** A person sees the recipient and the content and confirms before anything leaves, and can review AI-written notes and bills before use.

**Nevada today (Assembly Bill 406 (2025), sec. 8(4)).** The product shall give the provider a way to independently review, before use, anything the AI compiles, summarizes or generates for billing or for session notes.

Safeguards behind this question: AGENT-2, AGENT-3.

## 6. After something goes wrong, can anyone find out exactly what the AI did?

**Why it matters.** An AI system's own account of what it did is not a record. Without an independent log, nobody can investigate.

**A good answer comes with.** A log of every time patient data was read, sent or changed, kept so that edits show, with the AI's actions marked separately from the person's.

**Nevada today.** This project's overlay lists no state rule on this question. That is a statement about the overlay, not a finding that no rule exists.

Safeguards behind this question: LOG-1, LOG-2, AGENT-1.

## 7. Is one clinic's patient data kept apart from every other customer's, and has that been tested?

**Why it matters.** This is the most common serious flaw in new software, and no document can show it is absent. Only a test can.

**A good answer comes with.** A dated test in which one customer tries to read another's records and fails, repeated for each release.

**Nevada today.** This project's overlay lists no state rule on this question. That is a statement about the overlay, not a finding that no rule exists.

Safeguards behind this question: GATE-3, MEM-1, TEST-1.

## 8. Does the AI present itself as a clinician, or provide care itself?

**Why it matters.** A person in distress may not tell a chatbot from a professional.

**A good answer comes with.** Marketing and in-product text that never calls the AI a therapist, counselor or doctor, and a feature list limited to support tasks where the law requires it.

**Nevada today (Assembly Bill 406 (2025), sec. 7(1)).** The product, its marketing and anything the AI itself says shall not represent that the AI can provide professional mental or behavioral health care, that talking to it is a way to obtain such care, or that it is a therapist, counselor, psychiatrist, doctor or similar provider.

**Nevada today (Assembly Bill 406 (2025), sec. 7(2)).** The product shall not offer an AI system specifically programmed to provide a service that would be professional mental or behavioral health care if a person provided it.

**Nevada today (Assembly Bill 406 (2025), sec. 8(1), 8(2)).** AI features used by a mental or behavioral health provider shall be limited to administrative support such as scheduling, managing records, billing, analyzing operational data, and organizing or managing session files and notes, and shall not be used in providing care directly to a patient.

Safeguards behind this question: AGENT-5.

## 9. Does the tool work as well for every group of patients?

**Why it matters.** A tool trained mostly on one population can be less accurate for others, and nobody sees it unless results are broken down.

**A good answer comes with.** The inputs the tool uses and its measured performance for each patient group, given to the provider who uses it.

**Nevada today.** This project's overlay lists no state rule on this question. That is a statement about the overlay, not a finding that no rule exists.

Safeguards behind this question: CONSENT-6.

## 10. Who checked all of this, and can their work be verified?

**Why it matters.** No HIPAA certification exists, and "compliant" on a website is the vendor's own word. What matters is who tested what, on which version, and when.

**A good answer comes with.** Dated evidence for each claim, signed so that later changes show, and co-signed by someone independent of the vendor.

**Nevada today.** This project's overlay lists no state rule on this question. That is a statement about the overlay, not a finding that no rule exists.

Safeguards behind this question: TEST-2, TEST-4.
