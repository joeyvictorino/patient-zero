## NV-1 (AB406 sec. 7(1))

**Our statement of the requirement:** The product, its marketing and anything the AI itself says shall not represent that the AI can provide professional mental or behavioral health care, that talking to it is a way to obtain such care, or that it is a therapist, counselor, psychiatrist, doctor or similar provider.

**Who we say it applies to:** every AI product offered to people in Nevada.

**What we tell a clinic it means:** The AI must never present itself as a therapist or counselor, or as a way to get therapy.

## NV-5 (SB370 NRS 603A.500 to 603A.590)

**Our statement of the requirement:** Where the vendor handles consumer health data that HIPAA does not cover, it shall state whether it is exempt as an entity subject to HIPAA, and if not, shall publish a consumer health data privacy policy, obtain consent before collecting or sharing, honor requests to stop and to delete, and keep such data out of geofencing around health care facilities.

## NV-6 (NRS200 NRS 200.620, 200.650)

**Our statement of the requirement:** A feature that records or transcribes a phone or video call shall obtain the consent of every party before recording. NRS 200.620 bars intercepting a wire communication without one party's consent plus a court order or emergency, and the Nevada Supreme Court has read it to bar taping a call with only one party's consent (Lane v. Allstate, 1998; extended to cell phones in Sharpe v. State, 2015). It does not reach a recording made entirely outside Nevada (Ditech v. Buckles, 2017). In-person conversations under NRS 200.650 need one party's authorization; CONSENT-2 in the profile asks for every party's agreement in all cases.

**Who we say it applies to:** AI scribes, call summaries and any feature that records or transcribes conversations with people in Nevada.

**What we tell a clinic it means:** Nobody on a phone or video call may be recorded or transcribed by an AI tool unless everyone on the call has agreed first.
